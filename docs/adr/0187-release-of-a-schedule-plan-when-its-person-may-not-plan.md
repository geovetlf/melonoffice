# ADR-0187: A person's plan is released when they may no longer plan

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Amended: 2026-10-10, decisions 6 and 7 and limits 1 and 2. Geovet answered two decision cards in the project chat; the coordinator relayed both at 17:34Z. Decision 8 and limits 3, 5 and 6 follow his answer «Arreglar con consulta» in the thread «Diagnóstico del Agent Engine» (22:17Z), which confirmed the scope of the crash recovery.
- Builds on: [ADR-0029](0029-runtime-guards.md) (the runtime acts for the person, never more, and never cancels), [ADR-0185](0185-workflow-recurrence.md) (the standing permissions and the claim), [ADR-0186](0186-delegation-recovery-and-closure-of-off-schedules.md) (the closures this changes, and its limits)
- Product decision: Geovet, 2026-10-10 16:22Z, relayed by the project's coordinator in thread «Diagnóstico del Agent Engine»: a switched-off schedule's abandoned plans, approved ones included, are recovered or closed only under safe and auditable conditions, and a switch-off never cancels a live execution nor closes a plan before its recovery conditions are exhausted. A person who loses the permission to create plans stops holding a `creating` delegation, released once and audited, with no duplicate execution and no block of another organization. J1 stays blocked.
- Product decisions, project chat, 2026-10-10 17:34Z (decision cards):
  - «Sí, liberar»: the sweep also releases the hand-made plans of a person who may no longer plan (decision 6).
  - «Solo permisos»: the sweep decides on permissions only. A person who left the organization keeps their schedules and plans as they are (decision 7).
- Terraform: none. Migration: none. Index: none. The three sweep queries each have one equality, which the automatic single-field index serves (decisions 5, 6 and 8).

## Context

ADR-0186 holds a plan whose person may no longer plan: a refusal of `permission_denied` leaves it as it is (its decision 6). Such a person keeps the schedule blocked. The next claim overlaps the stuck plan, and the sweep reads it again on every run, until the person gets the permission back.

A person's plan made by hand has the same problem without a schedule. Its `creating` delegation can stop mid-way, and nothing but a person with `plan.create` can close it (`closeFailed`), so the plan stays open once that person loses the permission. No sweep looked at hand-made plans.

The sweep's query for lapsed claims filtered on two fields, `status` and `last.outcome`, and ordered by the document id. Firestore may need a composite index for two equality filters with that order. The emulator does not enforce indexes, so the tests could not show whether DEV needs one, and ADR-0186's "no index" line was an inference from the emulator.

A cleanup writes the plan's failure first and closes its planning execution in a second write. A crash between the two leaves the execution open. The plan is failed, so the sweep never reads it as `creating` again, and only a person with `plan.create` could close the execution.

## Decision

1. **The runtime releases a schedule's plan whether or not its person may still plan.** `Delegation.abandon`, `PlanService.abandonScheduled` and `Delegation.closeFailed` apply the rules of ADR-0186 unchanged: the schedule's own plan, the same workflow, an earlier occurrence that superseded it (or the schedule switched off), the plan untouched since before the lease, and the status and delegation state that closure needs. The only change is that the runtime does not need `plan.create` to read the plan for these closures. A release creates, starts and executes nothing, so it needs no permission the runtime lacks. A person's own closures still require `plan.create`.
2. **The audit names the lost permission.** When the person may not plan at the release, the `plan.state_changed` event carries reason `permission_lost` in place of the schedule's reason (`schedule_abandoned` or `schedule_off`). Its reference stays `occurrence:<occurrence>`. A release made while the person may plan keeps the schedule's reason. The permission is read in the same request as the release, so the audit shows the person's state at that moment.
3. **A claim closes what its person left, then refuses what they may not do.** Every claim releases the earlier plans (ADR-0186, decision 5) before its checks. It then refuses the occurrence `not_allowed` when the person may not plan (ADR-0185, decision 6), and plans nothing, because planning still requires `plan.create`. The sweep settles an off schedule's plans the same way (ADR-0186, decision 7). Once the person may plan again, the next claim takes the normal path.
4. **`permission_denied` no longer holds a plan.** ADR-0186, decision 6 is amended: the runner holds a closure only for `plan_not_abandonable`, the plan changed within its lease or started meanwhile. Any other closure failure fails the occurrence, which the task retries.
5. **The sweep's query has one equality and no composite index.** `lapsedOff` reads the claims whose `last.outcome` is `claimed`, in document id order, from a cursor: `startAfter` the last id of the previous page. The automatic single-field index on `last.outcome` serves it, so no composite index is needed and Terraform is unchanged. The schedule's `status` and its lease are read from each record. Open claims are few: one per schedule whose claim has not finished. A page may hold fewer settleable schedules than its limit, but `next` is still returned while the page is full, so the walk reaches every settleable one. The in-memory store follows the same semantics.
6. **A person's hand-made plan is released once they may no longer plan.** `Delegation.releaseManual` (runtime only) fails the `creating` delegation of a plan that no schedule made: no `decision.via` of `schedule`, and no workflow occurrence. It applies only when the runtime is the plan's creator's, the creator lacks `plan.create` at that moment, and the plan has not changed since before the lease. The plan's status is `ready` or `approved`, the two a delegation begins from, so it never started. The change is one `plan.state_changed` event, reason `permission_lost`, with no reference, written in the plan's own transaction. The runtime then closes the planning execution, as for a schedule's plan. The children it made stay `pending` (ADR-0029). A release already made is read back and writes nothing. The sweep (`createPermissionRecovery`) is built by the schedule runner from its `recoveryPlans` and conductor. It runs after the schedules and before decision 8's closures, so the execution sweeper runs it with the schedules' sweep. It reads the `creating` plans of every organization in id order, a page at a time: 50 per page, 10 pages per run, from the last id of the page before. Each plan is judged by its own organization. A creator with an active membership and no `plan.create` is released; a creator who still may plan, or who has no active membership, is left as it is. The sweep's query has one equality on `delegationState`, so it needs no composite index either. Approved plans with no delegation are not released: nothing holds them, starting one needs `plan.create`, and listing every approved plan would read them all on each run, not only the stuck ones.
7. **A departed person's schedules and plans are not released.** Decided by Geovet in the project chat (17:34Z): the sweep decides on permissions only. A person without an active membership has no runtime (ADR-0029), so their schedule's occurrences are recorded `not_allowed` and their `creating` plans stay open, until the membership returns or a person acts. Nothing in the code changes for them: the sweep reads their plans on each run and leaves them as they are.
8. **The runtime closes a planning execution a crash left open, once a lease has passed.** `Delegation.closeAbandoned` (runtime only) closes the planning execution of a plan the runtime abandoned, one whose `delegationFailure` is `delegation_abandoned`, which only decisions 1 and 6 write. It acts only when every check holds, each read before any write: the creator's runtime has an active membership (ADR-0029); the plan failed at least a lease before `untouchedBefore`; the planning execution is still open; and no child is in an open status other than `pending`. A `waiting_approval` child holds it too, since it can resume from `running`. A `pending` child never started and does not hold it, as `abandon` and `releaseManual` leave it. The closure is the change `cleanUp` makes, the execution failed with the plan's failure code, so its audit reads as a normal cleanup. Nothing is cancelled, started or written to the plan, and a second attempt writes nothing. A plan that failed for another cause is never closed here: its cleanup stays with `closeFailed`. A person never closes through this path (`permission_denied`). The sweep (`createAbandonedRecovery`) reads every page of `abandonedPage`, by id, with one equality and no composite index, and asks each plan's creator's runtime to close it. It runs last in the runner's recovery, after the releases, and logs `abandoned plan recovery failed` with the store's code when it fails. Each plan is judged by its own organization. A creator who left has no active membership and is left as it is (decision 7).

## Limits

1. **Decided, not open: a departed person's plans stay open** (decision 7). A person who left keeps their schedule's and their hand-made `creating` plans until the membership returns or someone with `plan.create` acts. Their plans accumulate, and each run reads them again (limit 4).
2. **Closed by decision 6, with one residual.** The sweep releases a hand-made `creating` plan once its creator lost `plan.create`. An approved plan with no delegation is not released: nothing runs from it, and it stays `approved` until a person with `plan.create` acts or it is cancelled.
3. **The first DEV sweep is the index check, for three queries.** `lapsedOff` (`last.outcome == claimed`), `creatingPage` (`delegationState == creating`) and `abandonedPage` (`delegationFailure == delegation_abandoned`) each have one equality. The emulator does not enforce indexes, so DEV decides whether all three run without a composite index. If DEV asks for an index for `lapsedOff`, the schedules' pass fails with the store's error, as it did before, and the hand-made pass and decision 8's pass do not run in that sweep. If DEV asks for one for `creatingPage`, the schedules' pass settles as usual; the hand-made pass logs `hand-made plan recovery failed` with the store's code on each run, and releases nothing until the index exists. If DEV asks for one for `abandonedPage`, decision 8's pass logs `abandoned plan recovery failed` with the store's code on each run, and closes nothing until the index exists. Each index would then be written as Terraform in a later change, and the owner applies it.
4. **A run reads the held plans again.** A run reads at most 500 open claims and 500 `creating` plans, and a plan that is held (its creator has left, or still may plan) is read again on every run. Many held plans can delay the rest, as ADR-0186's limit 4 says. Open for the owner if the count grows: the held plans are the ones decision 7 keeps.
5. **Closed by decision 8.** A crash between a cleanup's two writes leaves the planning execution open until a sweep closes it, once a lease has passed and after the checks of decision 8. Until then only a person with `plan.create` could close it.
6. **The sweep reads every abandoned plan on each run.** `abandonedPage` has no bound, and a closed plan keeps `delegation_abandoned`, so each run reads every such plan of every organization, and for each one past its lease, reads its execution too. A bounded walk would read the same first pages on each run, since nothing marks a plan as closed, and starve the rest. The count grows with each stuck plan the runtime abandons. Open for the owner if the count grows: a stored cursor, or a closure marker written with the cleanup, which would change the plan's audit.

## Consequences

- A person who loses `plan.create` no longer blocks their schedule or their hand-made plans. The next claim or the next sweep releases the stuck plan, audited once, and the occurrence is refused until the permission returns.
- A departed person's stuck plans stay open by decision (limit 1). They accumulate, and each run reads them again (limit 4).
- All three sweep queries need no composite index, by decisions 5, 6 and 8. The emulator cannot prove that, so DEV's first sweep after the deploy does (limit 3).
- A cleanup a crash left open is closed by the sweep once a lease has passed, not at once (decision 8). The reads of abandoned plans grow with their count (limit 6).
